## Linear Functions and their graphs

Once we start plotting the graphs, we can see some clear patterns. For example, a linear function will always have its graph as a straight line (its direction, etc depends on the equation).

Instead of running away with other types of functions, I will now stop my car and zero in on linear functions.

A linear function is defined as:

$f(x) = ax+b$

For example, we have $f(x)=3x-4$. Plotting it would draw a line. Take another function, say $2x-1$ and it will also draw a line. Whenever we plot a linear function, it will have a linear graph. We will use [Desmos Calculator](https://www.desmos.com/calculator) for its ridiculously simple interface and ease of use.

### Effect of parameters on the graph

We can even infer the line from the function alone. If you notice the line’s equation again, $x$ is the input, which can be anything – an independent variable; its $a$ and $b$ which determine the function/line. They are known as parameters. Parameters are always fixed in a specific function (like 2 and 8 in the line we just drew). But we can play around with their values and study their effects. Let’s break free from pen and paper and use our eyeballs.

#### Effect of a

The coefficient of x, $a$, is a **scaling factor** — it determines how a change in the value of $x$ affects the function’s output (value of $y$ on the graph).

If $a$ is 1, then any increase or decrease in the value of $x$ will equally reflect in the output of the function. If $a$ is greater than 1, then the output will have a bigger effect — for example, **if a is 3, then there will be a three-times increase/decrease in the value of the output and graph will have a higher slope**. Similarly, if $a$ is positive but less than 1 (like 0.5), our graph will have a smaller slope. It’s an exercise for you to determine the effect of $a$ on the graph if its value is negative.

#### Effect of bias term

The constant term $b$ also has a role of its own. It can shift our line forward or backward (without changing its direction/slope). If b is negative, our line will be in the right half-plane and vice versa. 

**for the positive values of $x$*

- Quadratic (or polynomial of higher degrees)
